Rule 1

In this town, the trend is real

Every video opens with a town rule, then a kid’s story, then a twist at the end. That format is everywhere right now: YouTube compilations, TikTok how-to guides like this one, story generators, and even an “In This Town” game on Roblox.

  • It’s the first coin with this name on Solana. Launched on pump.fun Oct 29, 08:59 UTC. A Meteora namesake (4M7Xcmdpz9soSk3zura1NsWngCxLQHrsUAbk661TKbt2) came 3 minutes later and is dead. A fresh copy in the same RBLX pair appeared today (4ERxy4K8ppqaSYzrg3JECabUyFptePUmwWZDRNy7pump) with 2 buys.
  • Nobody owns the trend. There’s no creator account that could post about the coin. On X, ordinary people write about the videos and get 19–28 likes (1, 2).
  • The “billion views” line is the coin account’s own claim. The like counts on TikTok I took from search results and didn’t check by hand.

What’s left is a community takeover. The X account @RichInThisTown (“In this town, everyone gets rich.”) was created Nov 15, 18:25 UTC, in the middle of the run, and had 44 followers this morning. There’s also an X community and a TikTok @richinthistown.

GMGN’s summary says “dev team holds 7.75%”. All six dev-tagged wallets and AMeXQz2S hold 0. I couldn’t find who that 7.75% is.

Rule 3

In this town, the price is in Roblox stock

The pool pairs $Town with RBLXDGRD64AtRamHMFVcjqne3Ar7NLWtFtYNtsrf1cE, a tokenized Roblox share. So the dollar price of $Town is its price in RBLX times the price of RBLX. A red day for Roblox on the stock market pulls the market cap down even if nobody sells the coin. And anyone who sells gets RBLX, which is a second trade before it’s SOL or dollars. I didn’t check how deep the RBLX pools are.

The account’s newest pitch leans into this: holder rewards raised from 0.05% to 1%, “get rich in $RBLX simply by holding $Town”, waiting on pump.fun to approve. 2 likes when I looked.

Past coins paired to a “trend owner” asset in my notes: the Nvidia-paired BFRG had four waves to $171–176K and each one gave back 70–90%. The pair brought volume, not holders.

The pool holds 21.7% of supply, about $7.9K of tokens against about $7.9K of RBLX. If the top 10 sold everything into it (constant product, fees ignored), they’d get about $4.5K, 42 cents on the dollar, and the market cap would land near $6.6K. A $3–4K sell alone is already −30–40%. And whatever comes out is RBLX, not SOL.

Nobody is dumping right now: the biggest holders mostly haven’t sold since they bought. The point is the size of the door, not the mood.
